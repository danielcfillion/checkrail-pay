## Checkrail Pay security audit, round four (verification pass), 27 Oct 2026

### 1. Executive summary
- This was a focused re-test of the four findings I raised on 27 Oct (H1, H2, M1, M2) plus the new surface the fixes brought in. I built the round-four copy (HEAD daf268c), seeded it, ran the production server on :3105 with five role logins and personas on, attacked each fix over HTTP, and stopped the server by PID.
- All four hold. The login layer now works at runtime: the real login is recorded, and the persona switch no longer buys a second approver or a second signature. Reversal refunds are tied to the original payout by PayPal's Reference Txn ID and can only be applied by someone who reconciles; every forged, reused, cross-vendor and double-line variant I tried was listed as unmatched rather than applied. A clerk can no longer change a vendor's country. The dual-approval path now works without weakening the control: a high-limit approver signs first, a second approver (even a capped one) adds the required second signature, and one person still cannot provide both.
- I found no new vulnerability and nothing regressed. The new code paths (runAs, the second-signer rule, repayRefunded/repayAction, the Reference Txn ID binding, the setObligationDates lock, the execute-time reload and re-quote, the tightened evidence supersede filter) behaved as intended under the probes. The full test suite passes (298/298) and the build is clean. Security headers, enforced auth, the public/internal action boundary and the G4 self-attested-document block are all still in place.

Checks: `npx vitest run` passes 36 files, 298 tests; `npm run build` is OK. Server ran on :3105 with `CHECKRAIL_USERS` = owner(admin), appr and appr2 (approvers, default USD 1,000 limit), clerk, acct(accountant), and `CHECKRAIL_DEMO_PERSONAS=1`; stopped by PID. **H1 (Fixed).** The fix replaces the broken `enterWith`-in-a-helper with `runAs(login, fn)` (`AsyncLocalStorage.run`, requestContext.ts:15-17), and every server action now runs inside `inRequest` (actions.ts:43-54), which reads the real login from the persona header (falling back to the actor header) and runs the whole action body under it. Pages do the same through `getViewer().login` wrapped in `runAs` (viewer.ts:31, used across the page tree). Verified on :3105 with personas on:
- The login is now recorded: after an owner approval the `approvals.login` row reads `owner` (it was null in round three).
- Persona dual-approval refused: owner approved a USD 6,000 payment, switched to the Daniel persona and approved again; refused with "You have already approved this; the next approval comes from a different person". Only one approval stands.
- Persona-as-approver refused: owner switched to the Minh persona (an approver) to try to be the second signer; refused, because the real login behind the persona is still `owner` and owner's own prior approval does not count as the covering approval for a second human.
- Separation of duties by login: an obligation prepared by owner-as-persona-lan is now treated as prepared by `owner`, so owner cannot also approve it when another approver exists (the round-three bypass).

**H2 (Fixed).** A reversal is tied to a payment only through PayPal's Reference Txn ID, matched to a payout line this business already matched to one of its own settlements, and the amount, currency and sender (From) must also agree; one reversal refunds at most one payment, and only someone who reconciles (accountant, approver, owner) applies it (books.ts:159-182). Verified on :3105:
- Old attack, a reversal matching amount and email with no Reference Txn ID: listed as unmatched ("the line names no original transaction"), the payment stayed settled.
- Reversal citing a transaction id never matched here: unmatched ("it reverses a transaction not matched to a payment here").
- Legitimate tie (a payout line that sets the settlement's statement ref, then a reversal citing that id): refunded correctly, applied true.
- Cross-vendor: a reversal citing vendor A's payout id but with vendor B's amount and sender: unmatched ("the amount, currency or sender does not match that payment").
- Two reversal lines for one payout in one file: exactly one applied, the second listed as unmatched; the payment refunded once.
- Reuse of a reference id after the payment was already refunded: reported in the summary but not applied again (the apply step skips any settlement not in `settled`), so no double refund.
- Clerk upload of a legitimate reversal: listed with applied false; the payment stayed settled, so a preparer cannot trigger a refund.
- repayRefunded/repayAction cannot double-pay: the refunded obligation is terminal; "Pay it again" creates a fresh obligation that runs through full policy (it landed in awaiting_approval, require_approval, not auto-pay, because an invoice is still the vendor's own claim), a second repay is refused ("Already requested again"), and a clerk cannot execute it without an approval. A bill-linked refund is refused and sent back to the bill (read; no bill-linked refund existed in the demo to exercise, but the guard at orchestrator.ts:285 is unambiguous).

**M1 (Fixed).** `country` now joins tax type, tax form, withholding, phone and retainer behind `verify_vendor` (vendors.ts). Verified: a clerk changing a vendor to CA, and a clerk changing the tax form, were both refused ("Country, tax form and withholding change how much tax is kept back; an approver or the owner records them"); an approver could make the change.

**M2 (Fixed).** Above the dual amount the second signature is now a check rather than the authority: once an approver whose limit covers the amount has approved, any other approver may give the second approval (`coveringApprovalBy`, approvalGate at orchestrator.ts). Verified: owner approved a USD 6,000 payment, then the capped approver `appr` (USD 1,000 limit) gave the second approval and it moved to approved, with two distinct logins recorded. A capped approver trying to sign first was refused, and one person still cannot sign twice, so two different people are still required. The deadlock is gone without weakening the two-person rule; the warning now fires only when fewer than two people can approve at all.

**L1 (Fixed).** The supersede filter now replaces a system (integration) record only with a revision carrying the same invoice number, and never replaces job sheets, bookings or purchase orders (orchestrator.ts:289-294). By construction a vendor upload with a different reference can no longer displace the business's own invoice record.

### 3. New surface probed, and new findings

No new findings. The areas the coordinator asked me to probe behaved correctly:
- `runAs` and the persona header (actions.ts:43-54, viewer.ts:31): the login is set once around the whole action/page and survives awaits; I could not make a persona be counted as a second person anywhere (approval, second signature, separation of duties, vendor verification).
- Reversal binding by Reference Txn ID (books.ts): forged, reused, cross-vendor and two-line reversals were all rejected; see H2.
- `repayRefunded` / `repayAction` (orchestrator.ts:282-303, actions.ts:217-228): no double-pay, duplicate-guarded against itself, bill-linked refunds refused, clerk cannot pay without approval.
- The second-signer rule in `approvalGate` (orchestrator.ts): one person cannot satisfy both signatures; a capped approver cannot sign first; a persona cannot be the covering approver for its own login.
- The period-lock checks in `setObligationDates` (orchestrator.ts): a date in a closed period is refused for both the old and the new value, and a future invoice date is refused; a valid change is allowed.
- The execute-time reload and re-quote in `executeObligation` (orchestrator.ts): execution reloads the obligation after policy recomputes tax and re-quotes when the amount to send differs from the quoted amount, so the settlement records what actually goes; the in-lock payout-hash recheck and the open-attempt guard are unchanged, and my state-machine and double-pay probes found nothing.
- The evidence supersede filter (orchestrator.ts): see L1.

Two cosmetic notes, neither a vulnerability and neither new money exposure:
- A reversal whose Reference Txn ID points at an already-refunded payment is still counted in the reconciliation summary's `returned` list even though it is not applied; the apply step correctly skips it, so there is no double refund. Worth hiding from the count so a reader is not misled.
- A clerk can still run `reconcilePayPal` (it records the reconciliation and marks matched payout lines reconciled); only the refund is withheld from a clerk. This is acceptable: a clerk cannot apply a refund, and setting a settlement's statement ref requires a genuine payout-line match, so it does not open the H2 path.

### 4. Regressions
None found. The full suite passes (298/298), the build is clean, auth is still enforced (401 without credentials), the security headers are still sent (CSP, X-Frame-Options: DENY, frame-ancestors none, and the rest), the public/internal action boundary still refuses internal actions on public paths, and the G4 block (a self-attested document needs a person unless a job sheet, booking, purchase order or rate-card match supports it) still holds.

### 5. Ratings
- Overall security: 7/10. Back up from 6. The two High findings from round three are genuinely closed and verified over HTTP, the clerk tax-jurisdiction lever is shut, and the dual-approval control works without a shortcut. The money core remains strong and nothing regressed. It is not higher because the residual design items stand (period-lock local-day bucketing, the single-process heartbeat, CSP inline script, and the still-latent multi-tenant scoping), and because the controls are young and have now been rewritten three times, so they warrant continued regression tests around the login layer.
- Fit to run the hosted hackathon demo: 8/10. Back up from 7. Dual control, separation of duties and maker-checker now hold even with the demo personas on, the audit names the real login behind a persona, reversals and repay behave correctly, and no real money is at risk on mock rails. The residual shared-instance concern between simultaneous judges is unchanged and handled by the idle reset.
- Fit to move real money for a small business today: 5/10. Up from 3. The blockers I named are addressed: the login layer is real, reversals are bound to the original payout and gated to a reconciler, tax jurisdiction is behind the checker, and the dual-approval deadlock is resolved. Before real money I would still want the Wise live path exercised against a real account, tax-form documents with expiry (still open from the industry list), period-lock bucketing made unambiguous, and the single-process autopilot heartbeat moved off the web process, but the controls themselves now behave as advertised.
