## Checkrail Pay security audit, 26 Oct 2026 (re-audit)

### 1. Executive summary
- The team has rebuilt the whole top layer: named logins with roles, demo personas, a vendor master with maker-checker payout changes, public onboarding/evidence/remittance links, payment runs, bills, FX, Wise and bank-file rails with field encryption, bank-file download, Xero bills, reconciliation, period lock and tax withholding. Most of my 18 earlier findings are fixed or materially reduced.
- The money core is still the strongest part. I re-ran the race, idempotency, state-machine, payout-hash and double-pay probes against the new runs and bank-file paths and found no double-pay and no state bypass. Maker-checker on vendor payout changes is genuinely enforced: a proposer cannot verify their own change while any other verifier exists, and I confirmed this over HTTP.
- The real controls, though, are undermined by the identity model around that core. A single admin login, using the demo personas that render.yaml turns on, can satisfy a two-person approval and separation of duties by itself, and the audit log records the second approval under the persona with no trace of the real login. I reproduced this on a greater-than-5,000-dollar payment: one human approved as `owner`, switched to the `daniel` persona, approved again, and paid.
- With personas off (the live-money case), the default team shape cannot pay anything above the 5,000-dollar dual-approval threshold at all: only an admin has a limit above it, and one person cannot approve twice, so those payments deadlock. I reproduced the deadlock.
- A clerk (the preparer) can change money-affecting vendor fields with no second person: the tax form on file and the withholding rate. Asserting a W-9 removes 24 percent US backup withholding; lowering the Philippine rate reduces what is kept back. A clerk can also rename a vendor onto another vendor's exact display name.
- Checks: `npx vitest run` passes 274/274, `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean, `npm run build` is OK. I ran the production server from my copy on :3101 with the four role logins and personas on, attacked it as each role and unauthenticated, then stopped it by PID. Harness in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/11b88efa-03f4-5292-ba70-6074dd94746e/scratchpad/sec2/py/h.py`.

### 3. Details

**G1 Personas defeat dual control and hide the acting login (High for the demo's integrity; not reachable with live money)**
- What is wrong: `CHECKRAIL_DEMO_PERSONAS=1` (set in render.yaml) lets an admin login act as any demo persona. The persona becomes the audit actor and gets the persona's role and limit. Approvals, separation of duties and dual approval all count distinct `actor` strings, so one human with an admin login can produce two distinct approvers.
- Reproduced: on a 6,000-dollar payment (above the 5,000 dual-approval threshold) I approved once as `user:owner`, called `setPersonaAction("daniel")`, approved again as `user:daniel`, and the payment moved to `approved` / `auto_execute`; I then paid it as `daniel`. Two approvals, one person. The `approval.granted` and `execution.started` audit rows read `user:daniel` with no field naming the `owner` login behind them. `Actor.viaLogin` exists in memory but is never written to the audit detail.
- Impact: on the hosted demo the dual-control and separation-of-duties story is theatre: a judge logged in as owner can approve-twice and pay. It is gated to mock rails (`personasAllowed` returns false for `PAYPAL_ENV=live` or `STELLAR_NETWORK=public`), so it cannot move real PayPal or public-Stellar money. Related: the demo seed writes persona users (daniel, minh, lan, thu) into the `users` table; `approversFor` and `otherVerifiers` count them even though nobody can log in as them without the owner persona switch, so a real deployment that ever ran the demo seed would count phantom approvers.
- Fix: record the real login (`viaLogin`) on every persona-made approval and audit row, and exclude one human's persona identities from the distinct-approver and distinct-verifier counts. Never seed persona users into a database that also holds real logins. Keep personas off unless explicitly a throwaway demo.

**G2 No payment above the dual-approval threshold can be made by the default team (Medium)**
- What is wrong: for an amount over `dualApprovalThresholdMinor` (5,000 dollars), policy needs two distinct approvals. `approvalGate` enforces each approver's limit, and a person can approve only once. New approver logins default to a 1,000-dollar limit (`DEFAULT_LIMIT`), admins to no limit. So unless two people have a limit at or above the amount, the payment can never reach two qualifying approvals.
- Reproduced: 6,000-dollar payment. `appr` (1,000 limit) is refused for being over limit; `owner` approves once; a second owner approval is refused ("already approved"); `appr` is still over limit. The obligation is stuck in `awaiting_approval` with no path forward except raising a limit, lowering the threshold, or the persona trick in G1.
- Impact: the product advertises dual control, but a business with one owner-admin and capped approvers can never pay a large invoice through the app. The pressure is to weaken a control (raise limits, drop the threshold) to get work done.
- Fix: in Settings, warn when fewer than two active people can approve above the dual-approval threshold, and make it easy to grant a second high limit. Do not let the only escape be the persona switch.

**G3 A clerk changes money-affecting vendor fields with no second person (Medium)**
- What is wrong: `updateVendorProfile` needs only `edit_vendor`, which the clerk has. It writes `taxForm`, `withholdingBps`, `taxType`, `name`, `role`, `country` and `defaultAccountCode` straight onto the vendor with no checker. Tax form and withholding rate decide how much tax is kept back, that is, how much the vendor is actually paid. Only payout destination is behind maker-checker; these are not.
- Reproduced: as `clerk` I set the US creator's `taxForm` to `w9` (asserting a W-9 is on file, which switches off 24 percent backup withholding) and it was accepted with no verification. I also renamed a vendor to `Maria Santos`, colliding with an existing vendor's display name, with no error (the name-clash guard is only on create and verify, not on profile edit). I restored both.
- Impact: a single preparer can quietly stop tax being withheld (a compliance and money exposure), set a Philippine withholding rate to zero, or rename a vendor to impersonate another on screens, remittance advice and exports. None of it needs a second person or a document.
- Fix: put `taxForm`, `withholdingBps` and `taxType` behind the same checker flow as payout details, or at least require `verify_vendor`. Re-apply the name-uniqueness check on profile edit.

**G4 Self-attested documents still auto-pay small amounts in assist mode (Low–Medium, design; continuation of F6)**
- What is wrong: evidence from an operator or a vendor link raises a warning (R3b), but evidence with provenance `document` (an uploaded file whose extracted rows were left untouched, non-text) raises none. In assist mode a clean payment at or under the 250-dollar auto-execute threshold reaches `auto_execute` with no person. A supplier's own invoice is exactly a `document`-provenance row.
- Impact: a verified vendor's self-made invoice for 250 dollars or less, naming themselves, is paid automatically with no human review in the default assist mode. The autopilot path is now hardened (it requires a job sheet, booking, purchase order from the business's own systems, or a rate-card match: policy.ts:320-325), so this is only the assist auto-execute window, but it is still money out on the payee's own word.
- Note: I confirmed this by reading the policy and ingest code rather than over HTTP, because the multipart encoding of the `uploadEvidenceAction` FormData server action did not reproduce cleanly in my harness. The decision path is unambiguous.
- Fix: treat a payee-authored document as a claim, not proof, for auto-execute too: require an independent record or a rate-card match before any payment clears without a person, not only under autopilot.

**G5 Obligation actions are not scoped to the caller's business (Low; Info today)**
- What is wrong: `approveObligation`, `executeObligation`, `amendObligation`, `retryAmbiguousExecution`, `resolveManually` and `mustGet` look obligations up by id only, never by `businessId`. The app is single-business today, so there is nothing to cross into, but if it ever becomes multi-tenant this is an IDOR: any authenticated user could act on another business's obligation id.
- Fix: thread `businessId` into `mustGet` and the obligation actions now, while it is free, the way vendors, bills and runs already are.

**G6 Dev data key is derivable from the source (Low, by design, mock only)**
- What is wrong: when `CHECKRAIL_DATA_KEY` is unset and no live rail is configured, `key()` falls back to `DEV_KEY = sha256("checkrail-pay development key: never protects real data")`, a constant in the repo. Full bank account numbers (`bankAccountEnc`, bank-file `accountEnc`) are then "encrypted" with a key anyone can compute. `realMoneyPossible()` forces a real key for live rails, and render.yaml generates a random one, so this bites only a careless local or sandbox run.
- Fix: this is acceptable for a mock demo. For any shared or hosted instance, refuse to store a full account number under the dev key even in mock mode, or warn loudly on boot when `usingDevKey()` is true and any `bankAccountEnc` exists.

**G7 Wise is untested and the local-rail figures are modelled (Low)**
- What is wrong: `wise.ts` states in its own header that it has never run against a Wise account and that recipient field names and funding differ by region. The Wise and bank-file quotes come from a modelled price list (`localQuotes.ts`), and `settleFromRail` records those modelled fee and received figures as the settlement for everything except live Stellar. So the accounting "received" and "fee" numbers for Wise and the bank file are estimates, not provider-confirmed.
- Impact: not a breach, but for real money the reconciliation and books would carry figures the provider never confirmed, and the live Wise path is beta.
- Fix: label Wise live as beta in the UI (it is labelled in code), and reconcile Wise fees and received amounts from Wise's own transfer record before writing them to the books.

**G8 Period lock uses local-day bucketing (Low)**
- What is wrong: `assertOpenPeriod` compares a record's date, converted to `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh` (the default `TIMEZONE`), against the lock date. A plain `YYYY-MM-DD` is bucketed at noon UTC; a full timestamp is bucketed in +07:00.
- Reproduced: with the books closed through 2026-10-25, a bill dated 2026-10-25 was correctly refused, but amending an obligation created at 18:49 UTC on 2026-10-25 was allowed, because in +07:00 that timestamp is the 26th. The two paths disagree about which day a record belongs to.
- Impact: records created in the late-UTC part of the closing day escape a lock that names that day. Consistent with local-day accounting, but the mixed noon-UTC-versus-local bucketing is a latent off-by-one.
- Fix: bucket every record the same way (choose business-local day everywhere, including plain dates) so the lock boundary is unambiguous.

**G9 PostCSS advisories through next (Low, build/dev only)**
- `npm audit --omit=dev` reports PostCSS XSS and source-map path-traversal advisories reached only through `next`'s bundled `postcss`. These run at build and lint time, not at request time, and `npm audit fix --force` would pull `next@16` (a breaking change). Leave next at 15.5.28; the earlier server-action DoS advisory (old F14) is resolved by this version.

**G10 Amend still accepts `vendorId` (Low, was F17)**
- `validateAmendPatch` still accepts `vendorId`. `amendObligation` refuses to change the payee when the obligation already has one, so the exposure is only an obligation the reader could not match to a vendor; a clerk amend can then set any vendor, after which approvals are superseded and a fresh approval is needed. Same as before. Drop `vendorId` from amend, or require a second person when it is used.

### 4. Status of the 25 Oct findings

- F1 (regex and PDF DoS): Fixed. The amount regex is rewritten to be linear; a 19,000-character comma-run ingested in 0.07 s and did not stall a concurrent GET. Request text is capped at 20,000 characters. PDF text extraction now runs in a child process (`scripts/pdf-text.mjs`) with a 10 s timeout, a 192 MB heap cap, a 1 MB output cap and a 20-page limit.
- F2 (zero caps mean unlimited): Fixed. `setAutonomy` refuses caps below 1.00 and a cancel window below 60 s, and policy fails closed when a cap is zero or missing. Confirmed over HTTP.
- F3 (fail-open auth): Fixed. With `CHECKRAIL_USERS`/`CHECKRAIL_BASIC_AUTH` unset, the middleware returns 503 in production or with live rails, and open mode needs `CHECKRAIL_INSECURE_DEMO=1`. A malformed setting returns 503. With logins set, every page is 401 without credentials (confirmed).
- F4 (boot-time wipe): Fixed. `assertSafeToReset` refuses when real money is possible or when the database holds any business other than the demo. The CLI seed goes through the same guard.
- F5 (one identity, no dual control): Partly fixed. Real named logins and roles now exist and below-limit dual approval works with two qualified approvers. But the default team deadlocks above the dual threshold (G2) and personas let one admin fake two approvers (G1).
- F6 (self-attested evidence clears autopilot): Partly fixed. Autopilot now requires an independent system record or a rate-card match. The assist-mode auto-execute window still pays a self-attested document under the threshold with no person (G4).
- F7 (CSV CR injection): Fixed. `csvCell` quotes any field with CR or LF and neutralises a formula after any line break; it is used by the accounting, Xero and bank-file exports.
- F8 (stale releaseAt): Fixed. Execution clears `releaseAt` inside the start transaction, and `retryFailed` clears it too, so a retried payment cannot inherit an old autopilot release.
- F9 (ingest not transactional): Fixed. The obligation, its evidence and attachment links are written in one transaction; evidence is validated (enum kind, safe-integer non-negative amounts, ISO currency, length and count limits).
- F10 (no security headers): Fixed. CSP, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, HSTS, nosniff, Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy are all sent; `X-Powered-By` is off. Confirmed on the wire.
- F11 (matcher prefix leak): Fixed. The matcher now excludes only `_next/static/`; `/favicon.icox` and `/_next/imageX` return 401 (confirmed).
- F12 (webhook unbounded body): Fixed. The route reads at most 64 KB, requires the PayPal signature headers in live mode before reading, and returns 404 in mock mode.
- F13 (settlement audit growth): Fixed. `confirmSettlement` writes a `settlement.checked` row only when the external status changed or the source is not the autopilot poll.
- F14 (dependencies): Mostly resolved. `next` is 15.5.28, which clears the server-action DoS advisory; the image-optimizer path stays mitigated. Remaining advisories are build/dev-only PostCSS (G9).
- F15 (memo side channel): Fixed. The memo uses the obligation reference only when an attached record carries that exact reference, otherwise the obligation id; agent-chosen text cannot reach the payee.
- F16 (uploads kept forever): Fixed. A 500 MB per-business storage cap is enforced on upload, and `purgeUnlinkedUploads` deletes unattached uploads older than 24 h on every heartbeat.
- F17 (amend accepts vendorId): Still open, unchanged (G10).
- F18 (tick CSRF): Fixed. `POST /api/autopilot/tick` requires either a bearer token or the custom `x-checkrail-tick: 1` header; a plain cross-site POST is 403 (confirmed).

### 5. What is done well
- Maker-checker on payout details is real and enforced server-side. An owner proposing a new PayPal address could not verify it while other verifiers existed; a clerk could not verify at all; only a different approver could. Payout details never change in place, and a brand-new vendor is blocked by policy until verified.
- The money core held every probe: compare-and-swap on the state transition and the approval race, refusal to execute while any attempt is not definitively failed, same-key replay on reconcile, the payout hash pinned at approval and re-checked inside the execution lock, and unknown outcomes kept apart from failures. Payment runs and the bank file reuse this path, so none of a single payment's checks are skipped in a batch; the bank file dedupes on the idempotency key so a retry cannot double-list.
- Role enforcement is consistent across the new surface: the clerk cannot approve, verify, set rates, release runs or pay runs; the accountant moves no money (export, reconcile, lock only); only the owner reopens a closed period. Every attempt I made to act above a role was refused with a clear message.
- Public links are sound: 24 random bytes, only the SHA-256 hash stored, single-use with a compare-and-swap spend, format-validated, and the onboarding and evidence actions refuse to run anywhere but their own public page. Server actions posted to `/onboard`, `/evidence` and `/remit` as an internal action all returned "Not signed in".
- Exports are safe against formula injection: `csvCell` neutralises leading `= + - @` and tab, and quotes CR/LF, in the accounting, Xero and bank-file files. The bank file carries full account numbers but is gated to the `pay` permission, never cached, freezes on download, and records who downloaded it.
- Caps and autonomy fail closed; the cancel window has a 60 s floor; autopilot will not send a first payment to a vendor or an amount far above the vendor's median.

### 6. Ratings
- Overall security: 7/10. Up from 6. The money engine is strong and most earlier holes are closed: hard caps, enforced auth with roles, security headers, DoS fixed, genuine maker-checker on payout changes. It loses points for the identity layer: personas let one admin counterfeit dual control and the audit does not show the real login, the default team deadlocks above the dual threshold, and a clerk can change tax, withholding and vendor identity with no second person.
- Fit to run the hosted hackathon demo: 8/10. Auth is enforced, roles and maker-checker demonstrate well, rails are mock, caps are hard, the DoS is fixed and the seed cannot wipe a non-demo database. The persona dual-control shortcut is a demo convenience on mock money, not a risk to anything real; the main residual exposure is still shared-instance state between simultaneous judges, which the idle reset and Reset demo address.
- Fit to move real money for a small business today: 4/10. Up from 3. Blockers first: the default team cannot pay above the 5,000-dollar threshold at all, a clerk can switch off tax withholding and rename vendors unilaterally, a self-attested invoice auto-pays small amounts in assist mode, Wise live is untested and its figures are modelled, obligation actions are not business-scoped, and the dev-key fallback and single-process heartbeat remain. Fix the approval-limit deadlock, put tax and withholding behind the checker, make auto-execute require independent evidence, and harden the Wise path before real money.
