## Bottom line

The round six Must-fix is fixed and I could not break it again in any order I tried. Seven of the eleven findings are fixed (PR6-1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9), PR6-7 is partly fixed, and PR6-8, 10 and 11 were left alone on purpose. Replaying my full Friday (ten requests, one run, one bank file, one statement) went through cleanly, and the new evidence and rate warnings tell the approver something specific instead of the old boilerplate.

The merged round introduced no money-path regression that I could find. It did produce a few honest-but-misleading screens and one reconciliation inconsistency, all of which fail closed:

1. The payment run's confirm button says "approving 4 that are waiting" and then approves none of them (PR7-1).
2. The Aged payables report now shows a clerk's recorded bill that Xero never receives, so it will not tie to Xero (PR7-2).
3. A guide paid over Stellar silently receives the network fee less than the invoice, and the remittance advice does not say so (PR7-3).
4. The new "reversal not dated before the payout" check is bypassed by uploading the same file a second time (PR7-4).

I am satisfied that no Must-fix item is outstanding. The Should-fix list below is what I would want closed before real vendors are paid, and the three deferred items are explained in their own section.

## The decisions the fixer took

**1. Keeping the "typed by operator" warning on bill-linked payments. Acceptable, but the stated reason is thinner than it sounds.** In `policy.ts` the Autopilot and Assist "independent support" test is `provenance === "system"` plus a job sheet, booking or purchase order, or the rate card. The warning is a separate rule. Bill-created evidence carries provenance "operator", so it would not count as independent even if the warning were removed. The warning could be dropped when the bill's amount has been checked (the code already does exactly this for the agent-confidence rule) without changing who can pay what. Still, I accept keeping it: an advance has no document behind it, and an approver reading "check it against the vendor's invoice" is the right prompt. The cost is that a payment carrying it is skipped by a run (see PR7-1) and will not be paid by Autopilot, which for advances and balances is what I would want anyway. The new wording ("from a bill recorded by Lan") is a real improvement.

**2. Stellar net equals gross because the fee comes out of the amount. Acceptable for the outlay column, not acceptable as a silent commercial default (PR7-3).** For the business's cash it is right: USD 750 left, USD 750 is netPaid. But it means Ana Reyes is owed USD 750, is told "Amount USD 750.00, You receive PHP 45,664.99", and the PHP figure is about USD 13.25 less than her invoice. I opened the remittance page: it does not mention a deduction. In a real company the guide phones the clerk. The books also show a USD 13.25 "fee" in the Fee column that the business did not pay, which a bookkeeper may add back as an expense. Either charge the Stellar fee on top like PayPal and the bank file, or say "USD 13.25 network fee deducted" on the advice and call the column "fee borne by vendor".

**3. Excluding the fee on a returned payout, on the assumption PayPal refunds it. Acceptable for PayPal, wrong for the bank file.** `paidSummary` drops the fee of any refunded settlement regardless of rail. PayPal returns the payout fee on an unclaimed payout (I believe so, and the statement's reversal line shows it); a Napas bank-file fee (VND 5,500) is not returned when a bank bounces a transfer. It is a few dollars a year, but the fee total is a number an owner reads. Take the fee from the reversal line of the statement when there is one, and keep it for a bank-file return.

**4. A recorded invoice with no payment requested yet counts as owed in aged payables. Defensible in principle, unsafe as built (PR7-2).** I recorded INV-KAL-1030 for USD 500 as Lan, nobody approved it, no payment was requested. It appeared at once in aged payables ("bill, unpaid", USD 500, total up by USD 500). The Xero bills file, which only receives approved bills, did not contain it. So the report an owner reads and the Xero payables a bookkeeper reads differ by every unapproved bill, and a clerk alone can add a liability line of any size to the owner's report. In Xero a draft or awaiting-approval bill is not in aged payables. Show unapproved bills as a separate "recorded, not approved" line outside the total, or count them only once approved.

**5. Letting a reconciler's upload tie a payout and its reversal in one pass. Acceptable.** The conditions I could test held: the sender, amount and currency must agree, a reversal for a payout not in the books is refused, one reversal refunds one payment, a clerk's upload applies nothing, and the same file twice does not refund twice. It also fixes the "upload twice" confusion I reported. Two cautions. The date check applies only to a payout matched in the same pass, so it can be defeated by uploading twice (PR7-4). And the sender check I could not isolate: my Rosa payout line did not match a book payment, so the forged reversal stopped earlier than the sender test. Both are reconcilers' risks, not a clerk's.

## New findings

### Must-fix

None.

### Should-fix

**PR7-1. The run's confirm text promises approvals it will not give.**
- *Did.* Lan built a run of four waiting payments (a vendor PDF invoice, a trip sheet with no departure, a clerk-typed invoice, a future trip). Minh opened it.
- *Saw.* The draft page said "4 still waiting for approval" and the confirm said "Pay 4 payments now, approving 4 that are waiting ... Yes, release USD 663.60". Releasing paid nothing: all four "skipped ... Has a warning a person must read first ... Open it and approve it there". The page ended "Nothing was paid."
- *Why it matters.* A run is the Friday ritual. The skip rule is correct (a clerk-typed record or the vendor's own invoice is exactly what a person should read), but nearly every real vendor invoice is "the vendor's own documents" unless a departure and a rate card cover it, so most of a real week will be skipped. The approver is told the opposite of what happens, and has to reopen every item one at a time.
- *Fix.* Count what will actually go ("Pay 5 now, 4 need you first"), and put an Approve button with the warning beside each waiting item on the run page so an approver can clear a run in one place.

**PR7-2. Aged payables includes bills the clerk recorded alone and Xero does not have.** See decision 4. *Fix:* separate line outside the total until approved, or count on approval.

**PR7-3. A guide paid by Stellar silently receives less than the invoice.** See decision 2. *Fix:* put the fee on the sender like the other rails, or state "USD 13.25 network fee deducted" on the remittance advice, in the chat message and in the Fee column heading.

**PR7-4. The pre-dated reversal check is defeated by a second upload.** A reversal dated 29 Oct against a 30 Oct payout was refused on the first upload and marked the payment Refunded on the second, because the payout was by then a prior-upload anchor and the check only covers same-pass anchors. *Fix:* apply the date check to every anchor. Allow one day of tolerance because the app shows Vietnam time and a PayPal statement is in the account's zone.

**PR7-5. The reverse-order duplicate edge.** An earlier request that is still "Needs evidence" counts as an original, so re-creating it with its evidence is Blocked ("The original is needs evidence") until the clerk cancels the first and presses Check again. It recovers, but the two steps in the wrong order is what the screen invites. *Fix:* a request that nothing supports yet should not block a request that does, or attaching evidence to the first should be the only path offered.

**PR7-6. Daily close still repeats the same job.** Two copies of Rosa's job were two "Blocked by policy" lines; the Phuong and Dao reversals were two lines. *Fix:* fold a blocked duplicate into one line with the original ("JOB-CEB2D-1024, 2 copies blocked") and a returned payout into the vendor.

### Nice-to-have

**PR7-7. The bank file merges two payments to one account into one transfer (VND 19,800,000 for HS-1027 and HS-1034) but the settlement records a fee of VND 5,500 for each.** If the bank charges one fee, the books overstate by one. Record the fee once per file line.

**PR7-8. Confirming a bank file is all-or-nothing.** "The bank paid it" marks every line in the file paid; a bank that rejects one beneficiary needs the per-line "Returned" afterwards. Offer per-line confirmation when a file has more than one line.

**PR7-9. A Daily close "Sign off" click did nothing I could see** (still "No sign-offs yet"). I did not investigate whether it needs a second step; I record it so a developer checks.

## Does the weekly flow still work for each role?

- **Clerk.** Yes. She creates requests, uploads PDFs, corrects an amount to the rate card, records bills and advances and requests their payouts, builds the run, downloads the bank file and chases vendors. What changed: she can no longer import the manifest (an approver or owner does; the Departures page says so), her Books upload shows matches and saves nothing (the page says so), and the Inbox opens on "Waiting for you", which for her is the blocked and needs-evidence requests, the right place to start. Nothing stalled.
- **Approver.** Yes. The approve button now reads "Approve USD 800.00 to Phuong Transport" with "Does not send money. Policy checks again, then it is ready to pay", a clearer contract than before. The manifest import re-checks waiting requests. The run confirm is the one misleading screen (PR7-1).
- **Accountant.** Yes. Statement upload, reversal tie, aged payables, settlement CSV and Xero bills file, period close and the late-invoice posting all behave (the 26 Sept invoice with September closed was posted as 2026-10-01 with the real date in the description).
- **A normal Friday run.** It does not stall if the approver approves items individually first, as in round six: all nine payments paid in one release, including Rosa's original, a stated-rate warning (USD 230 against USD 200) that Minh approved knowingly, the advance, both bank-file lines and the deposit. It stalls only for items that arrive with a warning or only the vendor's own support: those need to be opened one by one (PR7-1).
- **Not testable here.** The PayPal duplicate check that now warns when PayPal cannot be read runs only against live PayPal; on simulated rails it never makes the read, so I could not exercise it. The "rate card needs a finished trip" rule showed only as the future-departure trip sheet being held, which a typed-evidence warning would also have done, so I cannot say it was the cause.
