# Checkrail Pay: practitioner audit (AP operations, SE Asia tour operators)

**How I tested it.** I read the six docs plus `policy.ts`, `orchestrator.ts`, `schema.ts`, `actions.ts`, `record.ts`, `xero.ts`, `dailyClose.ts`, `queries.ts`, the ActionBar and the vendor and settings pages. I built the app, seeded it to `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/11b88efa-03f4-5292-ba70-6074dd94746e/scratchpad/ind/app.sqlite`, and drove it with Playwright on port 3102. I went through every page. I uploaded all four demo PDFs, and the outcomes matched the docs (Rosa clean, Bayan held, Tam blocked as a duplicate, Maria 5D clean). I paid Maria, approved and paid Ana, corrected Phuong to USD 800, cancelled the duplicate, turned on Autopilot (it sent Rosa and Maria 5D), opened Daily close and the sign-off confirm, and pulled the CSV. Then I tried three requests a real clerk gets in week one: a Vietnamese homestay bill in VND, a new driver nobody has set up yet, and a 50% hotel deposit. The server is stopped and the repo was not changed. Screenshots and scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/11b88efa-03f4-5292-ba70-6074dd94746e/scratchpad/ind/`.

**Bottom line.** Checkrail Pay reviews one payment request very well: the evidence, the deciding rule, the route and the history all sit on one page. It is not yet an AP function. A clerk cannot add a vendor, pay a VND invoice, pay a deposit, or run a weekly batch. Nothing reaches the books until money has moved.

## Findings from hands-on use

- **VND invoice is a dead end.** It is blocked with "Currency VND is not supported; payments are made in USD only" (rule R2b). Every Vietnamese homestay, driver and restaurant bills in VND. The seed avoids this by giving Vietnamese vendors USD contracts. That is rare in real life.
- **Unknown vendor is a dead end.** "Recipient is not a known vendor", and the only fix offered is "Correct to USD 105.00", which is the wrong remedy. There is no create or edit path for vendors anywhere in the app. `verifiedAt` is only ever written by `seed.ts`, so "payout details verified" cannot happen for a real vendor.
- **The deposit request becomes a full payment.** "50% deposit USD 425 now, balance on arrival" was read as USD 850, and approving it approved USD 850. Approval screens have no way to correct the amount (amend shows only for blocked items). Paying the balance later would collide with the same reference.
- **Rate drift is invisible without Claude.** Bayan at USD 160 a charter, against USD 130 historically, passes all 10 rules in rules mode. The invoice is its own evidence, so "Amount matches Invoice INV-BAYAN-0944" proves nothing.
- **Unusual-amount warning comes after the money.** Daily close flagged Rosa at "2.2 times usual" after Autopilot had already paid her. Under Autopilot this check should hold the payment, not report on it.
- **"Errors prevented" is a live count, not a running total.** It counts only items blocked right now. It dropped from $340 to $0 once I resolved the blocks, and it never counted Tam's USD 360 renamed duplicate. Owners will quote this number, so it must accumulate.
- **Seeded export dates are wrong.** The CSV shows bill and payment dates of 2026-10-25 for payments the ledger shows settled 8 to 17 Oct. Records show "exported csv:2026-09-30" but queued 17 Oct. An accountant will spot both in thirty seconds.
- **Docs and UI disagree.** Settings says a rail with missing credentials "falls back to mock"; the README says live mode never falls back. Settings shows `claude-sonnet-4-5`; the README says `claude-sonnet-5-5`.
- **False positive on deposits.** "Urgency language" fired on "deposit due now". Clerks learn to ignore warnings like this.
- **Daily close is in UTC and covers "since last sign-off", not a calendar day.** An operator in Hanoi or Manila closes in local time.

## 1. Missing tools and processes

**Vendor master and onboarding (missing; the biggest gap)**
- No vendor create or edit screen, no self-serve onboarding link, no KYC.
- No payout-detail verification: no callback log, no penny test, no PayPal account check before first payment.
- No second-person approval on bank-detail changes. That is the number one fraud vector in AP, and today it cannot even be tested.
- No vendor status (active or blocked), no contract or rate card, no document expiry (licence, insurance).

**Tax documents**
- Nothing. No W-9 or 1099-NEC for US contractors (Jake), no W-8BEN for foreign contractors.
- For a Vietnamese entity: no VAT e-invoice (hóa đơn điện tử) capture, and no 10% personal income tax withholding on payments of 2,000,000 VND or more to individuals without a labour contract.
- For a Philippine payer: no expanded withholding tax or BIR 2307.
- No tax code on a bill line, so a Xero import fails.

**Multi-currency and FX**
- Base currency only. A USD payment to a VND or PHP vendor leaves the FX spread (PayPal's 3%, modelled) with the vendor. That is how "you shorted me 600k" disputes start.
- Needed: pay in invoice currency, record FX gain or loss, and quote delivered local amount against invoiced amount.

**Partial payments, deposits, advances, credit notes**
- None of these exist.
- Tour operations live on hotel deposits and balances, cancellation credits, and guide cash advances for fuel, tickets and meals that are settled against receipts after the trip.
- Needed: an obligation with an invoiced total, payments applied against it and an open balance; plus a vendor balance ledger.

**Recurring payments**
- None. Monthly creator retainers, vehicle leases, office rent and SaaS have to be typed in each time.

**Payment runs and batching**
- One PayPal batch per obligation, one Pay click per item.
- No "pay all approved due by Friday", no run total, no funding check. The project's own live run hit insufficient funds.

**Approval matrix, roles and segregation of duties**
- One shared Basic-auth credential.
- "Preparer cannot approve" is enforced only in live mode.
- No roles (clerk, approver, admin, accountant read-only), no approvers by amount or category, no delegation while the owner is away, and no notifications. Approvers have to open the app to find out anything is waiting.

**Statement reconciliation**
- Polling each payment on its rail is not reconciliation.
- Missing: import of the PayPal activity report and the bank statement, matching against settlements, and a list of unmatched items (manual PayPal payments made outside the app, reversals, returned payouts).

**Period close, accruals and aged payables**
- A bill is created only at settlement, so the books are cash-basis.
- At month end, approved-but-unpaid and received-but-unapproved invoices are invisible.
- No aged-payables report, no cut-off, no period lock.

**Reporting for the accountant**
- Missing: cost per departure (no departure or tracking-category field), spend by account, fees and FX by month, and year-end 1099 totals.
- The CSV is not Xero's bill import template: the account column carries "310 Cost of Goods Sold: Contract labour" instead of "310", there is no TaxType, and there is no invoice date. The source invoice date in the PDF ("Date: 4 October 2026") is never captured.

**Vendor communication**
- No remittance advice saying which invoices were paid, the amount, and the delivered local amount.
- No "we need your trip sheet" request to a vendor.
- No vendor portal or Zalo/WhatsApp/email status updates. "Đã chuyển chưa?" (have you sent it yet?) messages will keep eating the clerk's day.

**Disputes, refunds and returns**
- The `refunded` and `disputed` states exist in the state machine, but no screen or flow reaches them.
- After settlement nothing is polled again, so a later PayPal reversal is never seen.
- No supplier refund on a cancelled departure.

**Evidence retention**
- Attachments are blobs in SQLite. The audit log is append-only by convention, with no hash chain.
- No retention policy and no per-payment evidence pack.
- The Xero adapter does not attach the invoice file to the bill, which is the first thing an auditor asks for.

**Intake and integrations**
- Paste or upload only. Despite the brief, there is no email forwarding, no Zalo, WhatsApp or Messenger intake, and no ingest API.
- No booking-system or dispatch link (Bokun, Rezdy, FareHarbor, TourCMS, Tourplan, or a Google Sheets dispatch board).
- No Wise or Airwallex, no bank bulk-payment file, and no QuickBooks or MISA.

## 3. Bottlenecks and fixes

1. **Vendor master.** In week one every new guide, rider and homestay blocks with no path forward. These items pile into "Needs a look" until the owner stops reading it. *Fix:* vendor create and edit screens, a self-serve onboarding link, and verification with maker-checker. Allow first payments with a low cap while verification is pending.
2. **Vietnamese vendors cannot be paid.** USD only, PayPal only. Most rural Vietnamese suppliers have no PayPal account and expect a VietQR or Napas bank transfer in VND. *Fix:* accept VND and PHP invoices; add Wise Business or Airwallex batch payouts to local banks; for Vietnam-based operators, export a bank bulk-payment file (Vietcombank, Techcombank and BIDV corporate banking all take one). Keep Stellar for the Philippines, where the benchmark says it wins.
3. **One approver, one sender.** On Assist, every clean item still waits for someone to press Pay. Anything over USD 250 waits for an approval, then a Pay. The owner is often on a trek without signal. *Fix:* payment runs (review the list, approve once, release), mobile approval links, a named delegate, and limits per approver.
4. **Unclaimed PayPal payouts.** These are guaranteed with new vendors and held correctly, but nobody is told and the 30-day return clock runs unseen. *Fix:* validate the PayPal account during onboarding, message the vendor automatically, show days to expiry on Daily close, and allow a re-send to a corrected address that goes through verification.
5. **Late evidence.** Trip sheets arrive days after the departure and on paper. *Fix:* treat the dispatch or booking record as the evidence; auto-create expected payables when the tour closes; let the guide confirm by phone photo.
6. **Rate drift and inflated invoices.** Without Claude, a higher rate passes. *Fix:* vendor rate cards with units (per day, per guest-night, per vehicle-day) and a deterministic expected-amount rule. This is cheaper and more defensible than a model.
7. **PayPal funding.** Payouts fail on a low balance. *Fix:* show the run total against the balance, with a funding alert before release.
8. **Month end.** The books only see payments, so accruals and per-departure cost are reconstructed by hand. *Fix:* post the bill at approval with a departure tracking category, post the payment at settlement, and add an aged-payables report.
9. **Deposits and credits.** Today there is a real overpayment path. *Fix:* invoiced total, payment amount, open balance, and credit notes.
10. **Close fatigue.** Items that cannot be fixed in the app stay in "Needs a look" every day. *Fix:* the vendor and currency features above, plus snooze-with-reason.

## 4. Market and fit

**Who would buy, and what they use today**
- **(a) Western outbound operators and small DMCs paying SE Asian suppliers in USD.** This is the product's current shape. They use Wise Business batch payments, PayPal mass pay, Payoneer, sometimes Airwallex, plus Xero with Dext or Hubdoc and a spreadsheet. The segment is real but small: many route supplier payments through a local DMC that pays in local currency.
- **(b) Operators based in Vietnam, the Philippines or Thailand paying domestic suppliers in local currency.** This is the large segment. They use corporate bank bulk transfers, VietQR, cash, Zalo, LINE or Viber, Excel, and MISA, plus their own dispatch spreadsheets. The product cannot serve them today; the docs concede the founder's own Vietnam payments are out of scope.
- **(c) Bookkeepers and accountants with several small travel clients on Xero or QuickBooks.** This is the right channel. They buy on clean bills with attachments, reconciliation and tax handling, not on routing.
- **Not the target:** Tipalti and Bill.com are too heavy and too US-centric for 10 to 60 suppliers. Bill.com's international payouts are not built for a Ha Giang homestay.

**What would make them switch**
- A duplicate or inflated invoice that hurt them. The demo nails this.
- An owner who can no longer approve everything personally. Autonomy and Daily close answer this.
- An accountant who wants clean bills with attachments.
- Visible savings against PayPal's 2% fee plus about 3% FX. To win that, the product has to route to Wise or a local bank where they are cheaper. Its own benchmark says Wise beats both PayPal and Stellar.

**Must have before a pilot**
1. Vendor create and edit, onboarding, and verification.
2. Named users with roles, and approval routing.
3. Local-currency invoices and at least one rail that reaches Vietnamese and Philippine bank accounts.
4. Payment runs.
5. Xero OAuth: bill at approval, attachment, tracking category, tax code.
6. Rate cards.
7. Hosted Postgres with backups, data-processing terms, and the operator's local timezone.
8. A working reconciliation report against the PayPal statement.

## What it already does well

- **Deterministic policy as the only authority.** Evidence binding requires the vendor's full name, and there is clear block, hold and approve logic. An auditor can follow it.
- **Duplicate detection.** It catches a renamed re-issue (INV-TAM-0834-B) from shared job identifiers. Most AP tools miss this.
- **Payout destination pinned at approval** and policy re-run at release. Idempotency keys, an "unknown means do not pay again" stance, and holding UNCLAIMED payouts are right and rare at this size.
- **"What you are deciding" panel.** Asked for, evidence says, goes to, route. This is the best single-payment approval view I have used in a small-business tool.
- **Autonomy design.** Caps, a cancel window, instant pause and the actor recorded on every send. "Autonomy removes the approval step and nothing else" is a sentence a controller can sign.
- **Daily close with an audited sign-off** that is refused if the page went stale. This is a genuine owner workflow.
- **Honesty.** The modes footer, a benchmark that admits Stellar loses below about USD 500 and Wise wins, and a clear list of what is mocked. Accountants trust that.

## Ratings

- **Usefulness to the AP clerk: 4/10.** It catches the errors that matter on a single payment, but the clerk cannot add a vendor, pay a VND bill, pay a deposit or run a batch, so most of the day stays in chats and spreadsheets.
- **Usefulness to the owner: 6/10.** Caps, Autopilot and Daily close are what an owner wants, held back by a resetting KPI, no cash or funding view, and UTC.
- **Completeness as an AP product: 3/10.** It covers capture-to-pay for one USD payment and lacks vendor management, tax, multi-currency, partials, runs, reconciliation and accruals.
- **Readiness for a real pilot: 2/10.** Vendors exist only in the seed, there is one shared login, it is USD only, it runs on SQLite, Stellar is simulated and Xero is not connected.
- **Strength as a hackathon entry: 8/10.** It has a real PayPal sandbox payout, a clean safety story, a polished and honest demo and 213 tests, docked for the simulated Stellar leg and a demo world (USD-billing Vietnamese vendors) that a judge with SE Asia experience will question.

**Worth saving to memory:** the audit's top pilot blockers are vendor management, local-currency (VND) payments with a Wise, Airwallex or bank-file rail, roles, and payment runs. The "errors prevented" figure resets to zero when blocks are resolved, and the demo CSV dates do not match the ledger.
