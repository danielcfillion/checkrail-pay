## Must-fix

| ID | Finding | What changed | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEC5-1 | A clerk alone could create the "independent support" for their own payment and send it with no approver | Importing departures now needs `verify_vendor` (approver or owner), the same as setting a rate card, so a clerk can neither add a departure nor raise the quantities on a real one. Departures page and README say who does it. | `rates.test.ts`: a clerk and an accountant are refused, expected amounts unchanged, an approver still imports; with no departure on file the payment still goes to a person |
| SEC3-1 | A clerk's statement upload saved a planted PayPal id that a later forged reversal could name | Only someone who reconciles (accountant, approver, owner) saves statement references. A clerk's upload shows its matches and saves nothing; the Books page says so. | `books.test.ts`: the clerk upload saves nothing; a reversal naming a clerk-planted id is listed, not applied |
| SEC3-2 | The same planted id blocked the real line from matching | Closed by the SEC3-1 change | `books.test.ts`: the genuine line matches after a clerk's planted upload |
| SEC2-9 | Contract: unbounded list of released spends could freeze the owner's controls | Spend is kept as one total per hour: at most 25 entries however many payments are released. A payment counts for 24 to 25 hours, never fewer, so the daily cap is never looser than a true rolling day. | `test.rs`: 300 tiny releases leave one entry, 40 hourly releases leave at most 25; fails with 300 entries without the change |

Not done for SEC5-1, by choice: the reviewer also suggested requiring a different login to send an unapproved auto-execute payment. In Assist mode the clerk sending cleared items is the design, so this is left for the owner to decide. With the departure gate in place the clerk no longer controls the support that clears a payment.

Residual risk for SEC3-1: the accountant, approver and owner are still trusted to upload a statement that really came from PayPal. Trusting only anchors read from PayPal's own API, and showing each refund to a second person, remain open suggestions.

## Accepted

- **npm audit.** `npm audit` reports 4 moderate findings and `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 0. All four are one esbuild advisory (GHSA-67mh-4wv8-2f99, esbuild 0.24.2 or older) under `drizzle-kit`, a devDependency that nothing in `src/` or `scripts/` imports. It concerns esbuild's dev server, which neither drizzle-kit's CLI nor this app starts, and the Next.js production bundle does not include esbuild. Not reachable in the deployed app. Accepted as a dev-only risk. npm's suggested fix would downgrade drizzle-kit to 0.18.1, which is wrong; wait for a drizzle-kit release that drops `@esbuild-kit`. The earlier postcss advisory is resolved by the override in `package.json`.
- SEC2-5, SEC5-11, SEC2-6, SEC3-3, SEC5-9, SEC5-12: accepted for the hosted demo as the reviewer labelled them. SEC5-9 (policy reads the clock) could be tidied later.
