The reviewer's view of the three decisions:

1. Clerk sends cleared payments: accepted in principle, and accepted in full once the end-date check exists (it does now).
2. No per-username lockout: accepted for the hosted demo. For real money, add a per-username progressive delay that never refuses a correct password; a 12-character minimum is length, not entropy. Not built; listed here for before live money.
3. Render address header: the diagnostic is accepted, but my claim that a shared key could only slow logins down was wrong. Fixed: with `CHECKRAIL_THROTTLE_SPARES_VALID=1` (set in `render.yaml` for the judging demo only) a correct password gets in even when the address is over its wrong-password limit. Off by default, so guessing stays throttled everywhere the login is a secret.

## Decisions taken on the open questions (29 Oct, by Claude on Daniel's instruction to decide)

1. **A clerk sending a payment that policy cleared in Assist mode: keep as designed.** The reviewer's suggestion (a different login must send an unapproved auto-execute payment) would end Assist mode's main use. With SEC5-1 fixed the clerk cannot change what counts as support that is not the vendor's: rate cards and departures need an approver or the owner, and system job sheets, bookings and purchase orders come from connected systems. What a clerk can still send without a person is bounded by the auto-execute threshold (USD 250 in Assist, USD 500 per payment in Autopilot) and the per-vendor 24-hour cap (USD 1,000). Revisit if round 6 or 7 finds another way for a clerk to fabricate support.
2. **No per-username lockout.** The hosted demo's login is one shared credential that is published to judges, so guessing it is not the threat; a lockout would be: anyone could lock the judges out. Compensating controls: the per-address throttle on wrong passwords, and, where real money can move, the 12-character minimum.
3. **Render client address: not set blindly.** Render's own article ([How Render handles DDoS attacks](https://render.com/articles/how-render-handles-ddos-attacks)) says all inbound traffic passes through Cloudflare and then Render's load balancers, tells apps to read `x-forwarded-for`, and names only `CF-Ray` as forwarded. It documents no `CF-Connecting-IP` or `True-Client-IP`, and its example takes the first `x-forwarded-for` entry, which a visitor can write. I could not confirm what the last entry is behind both hops. So `CHECKRAIL_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` stays unset, and an opt-in diagnostic (`CHECKRAIL_LOG_CLIENT_IP=1`, off in `render.yaml`) logs the address headers on a failed login so one wrong password on the deployed demo settles it. This first version of the decision was wrong about the worst case: a throttle key shared by every visitor lets one person's eleven wrong passwords lock everyone out for 15 minutes. Round 6 caught it; see the round 6 section for the fix.
