# Checkrail Pay security review, round 5

Reviewer: independent AI security analyst, fresh copy of the repo at commit acc039d, 29 Oct 2026.
No human security audit has been done on this system; this is another AI review.

## 1. Executive summary

I read the money path, the login and authorization layer, the vendor and reconciliation engines, both rail adapters, the PayPal webhook, the AI layer, the deployment file and the Soroban contract. I then built the production bundle, seeded a fresh database in my checkout, ran the server on port 3115 with five logins (owner, two approvers, a clerk, an accountant) and demo personas on, and attacked it over HTTP with a script that calls the real Next.js server actions. I stopped the server by PID when done.

What holds up. Nothing I tried reaches a protected action without a login. Every role boundary I probed was refused with the right message, including forged identity headers, a persona cookie from a non-owner, a cross-origin server action post, the `x-middleware-subrequest` bypass family and path-normalisation tricks. Dual approval, separation of duties and the persona defence (a persona never counts as a second person) all held. The four CONTRIBUTING rules hold in the code: the AI has no payment tool, policy is re-run inside `executeObligation`, every attempt carries an idempotency key, and the webhook only calls `confirmSettlement`. Bank account numbers are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM and I found no plaintext in the database file, the WAL, the audit log, the server log, any page I fetched or the accounting export. `npm audit --omit=dev` reports zero advisories.

What does not hold up. Two High findings let a single non-approver role cause money to move or be reversed without a second person:

1. **SEC5-1 (new, High).** A clerk alone can fabricate the "independent support" that the policy requires before a payment can go out without a person. By importing a departure (a clerk permission), uploading a PDF they wrote themselves (provenance "document", no warning) and pressing Pay (a clerk permission), I got a USD 225 payment to an existing vendor to settle with zero approvals. The same import also lets a clerk replace the quantities on a real departure and so raise the rate-card ceiling that approvers rely on.
2. **SEC3-1 / SEC2-2 (carried, High).** Still open and now reproduced end to end over HTTP. A clerk plants a PayPal transaction id in one upload; an accountant later uploads a file with a matching fake reversal; a settled USD 680 payment became "refunded". SEC3-2 (the planted id blocks the real payout's match) reproduces in the same run.

Other points worth your time: the payout hash stored for every vendor is an unsalted fast hash over the plaintext account number, which defeats the data key (I recovered a 10-digit account number from the hash alone in under one second, SEC5-2); the login throttle can be bypassed or turned into a targeted lockout by anyone who can write `X-Forwarded-For` (SEC2-3 re-test); and SEC2-5, SEC2-7, SEC2-8 and the contract items SEC2-9, SEC2-10 and SEC2-11 are all still open.

Label meaning used throughout. **Must-fix** means I would not let the system move real money until it is fixed. **Should-fix** means fix before wider use or before the relevant component goes live. **Accept** means the residual risk is documented and reasonable. None of the Must-fix items affects the hosted demo on simulated rails, except as a cautionary example.

Ratings (details in section 8): overall security 7/10, hosted demo 8/10, moving real money 4/10.

I am **not** satisfied that no Must-fix items are outstanding. For the hosted mock demo I have no Must-fix item. For real money there are two (SEC5-1 and SEC3-1/SEC2-2), plus the contract's SEC2-9 before that contract ever holds funds.

## 4. New findings

Ranked by severity. File and line references are to commit acc039d.

### High

#### SEC5-1: A clerk alone can create the "independent support" that lets a payment go out with no approver (Must-fix)

Where:
- `src/lib/engine/rates.ts:58-59` (`importDepartures` needs only `ingest`), `rates.ts:94` (an existing departure's services are deleted and replaced), `rates.ts:141-160` (`expectedAmount` trusts what was imported)
- `src/lib/domain/policy.ts:72-78` (only "operator" and "vendor" provenance raise a warning, "document" raises none), `policy.ts:180-190` (rate-card match), `policy.ts:346-347` (`independent`), `policy.ts:392-396` (auto-execute)
- `src/lib/engine/orchestrator.ts:144` (an untouched PDF gets provenance "document")
- `src/lib/auth/roles.ts:40` (clerk holds both `ingest` and `pay`; contrast `setRate`, which needs `verify_vendor` at `rates.ts:25`)

Impact. The G4 rule says no payment goes out without a person unless a job sheet, booking or purchase order from the business's systems, or the rate card, confirms it. A clerk controls every input to that test. The clerk imports a departure that lists a vendor, a unit and a quantity, which makes the rate-card amount whatever quantity times an approver-set rate they choose. They upload a PDF they wrote (the reader proposes a "job_sheet" row and, being untouched, gets provenance "document", which policy treats as clean). The payment then evaluates to `auto_execute` with no reasons, and the clerk presses Pay. One login, no second person, and the audit trail shows no approver at all. The payee must be an existing verified vendor, so this is insider fraud with a colluding vendor, bounded by the auto-execute threshold (USD 250 in Assist, USD 500 per payment in Autopilot) and the per-vendor 24 hour cap (USD 1,000). Because `importDepartures` can also replace the services on a departure that already exists, a clerk can raise the expected amount for a real departure (4 vehicle-days becomes 40), which removes the `expected_amount` block and shows approvers a green "Within the rate card" line on an inflated invoice.

Reproduction (mock rails, default Assist policy, threshold USD 250, logins as above):
1. As `clerk`, call `importDeparturesAction` with the CSV `code,start,end,guests,vendor,unit,quantity` and the row `HG-4D-9999,2026-10-01,2026-10-04,8,Maria Santos,day,5`. Response: "Imported 1 departure with 1 service." Maria's seeded day rate is USD 45, so the expected amount is USD 225.
2. Make a one-page PDF containing "Trip sheet HG-4D-9999. Lead guide Maria Santos, 5 days, USD 225.00 (JOB-HG4D-9999). Departure closed. Release payment." and upload it. (I called `uploadDocument` directly for this step, see section 2.) The reader proposed `kind: job_sheet`, amount 22500, reference JOB-HG4D-9999.
3. As `clerk`, call `ingestAction` with `sourceType: job_completion`, raw text "Trip HG-4D-9999 closed. Please pay Maria Santos USD 225 for 5 days guiding (JOB-HG4D-9999). Release payment." and that evidence row with its `attachmentId`.
4. Result in the database: `state=approved`, `policy_decision=auto_execute`, `policy_reasons=[]`, zero approvals.
5. As `clerk`, call `executeAction`. Response "Payment executed."; obligation `obl_7ce9e79b7f7d4d50` is `settled`. Audit actors: `user:clerk`, `policy`, `router`, `rail:stellar`, `system`. No approver anywhere.

Suggested fix, in order of value:
1. Put departure and service changes behind the same maker-checker as rate cards: require `verify_vendor`, or a second person, to create or change a departure that lists a vendor. Record who set it.
2. Never let the preparer be the sender of an unapproved payment: when a payment is `auto_execute` with zero approvals, require `pay` from a different login than the one who ingested, amended or imported the records behind it (the same login comparison already used for approvals).
3. Make provenance "document" raise the same warning as "operator" when the uploader is also the person who prepared the request (`attachments.uploaded_by` is already stored), or let only "system" provenance clear it.
4. Treat a rate-card match as independent only when the departure was created or changed by a login other than the preparer.
5. Qualify the README Autopilot claim until 1 to 4 are in.

### Medium

#### SEC5-2: The payout hash is an unsalted fast hash of the plaintext account number, so it defeats the data key (Should-fix, do before the first real vendor account is stored)

Where: `src/lib/domain/ids.ts:12-14` (`hashPayoutDetails`, SHA-256 truncated to 24 hex), `src/lib/engine/orchestrator.ts:388-392` (`payoutHashOf` feeds it `bankCode:accountNumber`), stored in `vendors.details_hash` (`vendors.ts:171, 261, 265`) and `obligations.payout_hash` (`orchestrator.ts:383`).

Impact. The README and `dataKey.ts` say full account numbers are protected by AES-256-GCM with `CHECKRAIL_DATA_KEY`. The ciphertext is fine, but the hash input contains the plaintext account number and the other inputs are known (bank code and masked label with the last four digits sit in the same row). Anyone who can read the database file or a backup, but not the key, can brute-force the account number from `details_hash`. Truncation to 96 bits does not help. Bank account numbers in Vietnam and the Philippines are 8 to 16 digits with structured prefixes, so the search is small.

Reproduction. Create a vendor with account `7283610456`, bank code VCB, verify the change as an approver. Read `vendors.details_hash` (`70bb316075b21cfd24d39437`) and `bank_label` (`VCB ****0456`) from SQLite. Loop over six digits: `sha256("||VCB ****0456|VCB:" + n + "0456")[:24]`. The account number was recovered in 0.8 seconds without touching `bank_account_enc` or the key. A 14-digit account with a known last four is about 10^10 candidates: minutes on a GPU, hours on a laptop.

Suggested fix. Compute the hash as HMAC-SHA256 with a key derived from `CHECKRAIL_DATA_KEY` (domain-separated, for example HKDF with the label "payout-hash"), and keep the same parts. Existing hashes need a one-time migration; verification is unaffected because the hash is only compared with itself.

#### SEC5-3: Login throttle key is client-writable; also collapses to one shared key without a proxy header (SEC2-3 re-test) (Should-fix)

Where: `src/lib/throttle.ts:20-27` (`clientIp`), `src/middleware.ts:43, 62-65`. `render.yaml` does not set `CHECKRAIL_CLIENT_IP_HEADER`.

Impact. The limit is 10 wrong passwords per 15 minutes per key, and the key is the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry. On a server that no proxy writes to, the client writes it. Reproduced locally (`next start`, no proxy):
- 10 wrong passwords from `X-Forwarded-For: 1.1.1.1` pass, the 11th is counted, and the correct password from the same address is then refused with 429.
- Rotating the header (`2.2.2.1` to `2.2.2.12`) gave twelve consecutive 401s and no 429, so guessing is unlimited.
- Writing a victim's address (`3.3.3.3`) eleven times with a wrong password made the victim's correct login return 429.
- A prefix such as `9.9.9.i, 4.4.4.4` is correctly ignored (last entry wins), which is the behaviour an appending proxy needs.
- With no `X-Forwarded-For` at all, `next start` filled the header with the socket address, and `X-Real-IP` was ignored. Every request then shares one key, so eleven wrong passwords from anywhere lock out everyone behind that address for 15 minutes (this happened to my own test client).

I could not establish how Render's edge sets the header. If its last entry is a shared edge or load-balancer address, the whole population shares a few keys and a single attacker can lock out all judges. Check Render's current documentation and a logged request before relying on either assumption.

There is also no per-username limit and no minimum password strength for `CHECKRAIL_USERS` (`basicAuth.ts:71-78` only checks the username pattern).

Suggested fix. Set `CHECKRAIL_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` to the header the host overwrites (after confirming it), add a second counter keyed on the attempted username, log lockouts, and refuse configured passwords under 12 characters when `RAIL_MODE=live`.

#### SEC2-8 (carried): Run release approves items in bulk without showing their warnings (Should-fix)

Where: `src/lib/engine/runs.ts:187`, `src/app/runs/[id]/page.tsx:108-109`.

`releaseRun` calls `approveObligation(db, ob.id, by.id, "Approved in payment run ...")` for every `awaiting_approval` item without `shownFingerprints`, so the "warnings the person saw" check falls back to the last evaluation on record, which nobody saw. The run page shows the state or a separation-of-duties note, never the policy reasons. A releasing approver therefore signs off warnings such as "vendor uploaded a document through their link", "same payout account as another vendor" or "names the payee only by part of the name" in bulk. Fix: show every item's reasons and amounts on the run page and require the approver to tick the items, passing the fingerprints they saw; or let a run pay only already-approved items.

#### SEC2-7 (carried): Look-alike folding misses most non-Cyrillic scripts (Should-fix, low-medium)

Where: `src/lib/domain/ids.ts:56-71`.

Reproduced with `foldConfusables` and `jobKeys` on `JOB-HG4D-0914` with one substitution. Armenian Օ (U+0555) or օ (U+0585), Cherokee Ꭺ/Ᏼ (U+13AA, U+13F4), Latin small capital ᴏ (U+1D0F), Coptic ⲟ (U+2C9F) all produce no job key at all, and `hasConfusables` returns false so no warning is raised either. A Devanagari zero (U+0966) keeps only the prefix `job-hg4d`. Cyrillic, Greek and full-width forms are folded and warned as intended. Effect: a re-invoiced job written with such a letter slips past the reference, job-key and PayPal-history duplicate checks. It cannot reach auto-pay because the rate-card and job-sheet routes read the same keys, so the remaining defence is the same-amount warning, which needs an unchanged amount. Fix: instead of a deny-list, flag any reference-shaped token that mixes ASCII with non-ASCII letters or digits (Unicode TR39 "mixed script"), and block, not just warn, when such a token appears in a request that otherwise matches a prior job.

#### SEC3-1 / SEC2-2 and SEC3-2 (carried, reproduced end to end) (Must-fix)

Where: `src/lib/engine/books.ts:133` (a clerk may reconcile), `books.ts:154` (match accepts a settlement whose `statement_ref` is empty or equal to the line's id), `books.ts:172-178` (anchors), `books.ts:182-183` (a reversal must name a stored `statement_ref`), `books.ts:206` (every matched line persists its transaction id, whoever uploaded it), `books.ts:208-218` (refund applied only if the uploader holds `reconcile`).

Reproduction (T5):
1. As `clerk`, call `reconcilePayPalAction` with a CSV whose payout lines match two settled PayPal payments by recipient email, amount, currency and date, using made-up transaction ids `PLANTA1` and `PLANTB1`. Response: "2 matched". Both settlements now carry `statement_ref` = the planted ids.
2. SEC3-2: as `acct`, upload a file with the genuine line for payment B under its real id `REALB1`. Result: 0 matched, `REALB1` listed as "on the statement only", i.e. money that left PayPal with no payment in the app. A false alarm that hides the real match.
3. SEC3-1: as `acct`, upload a file (in practice one received from the clerk) with a "Payment Reversal" line of the same amount from the vendor's email whose Reference Txn ID is `PLANTA1`. Result: `applied: true`; obligation `obl_1b3bf7c3ae504be7` (Dao Mountain Lodge, USD 680) moved from `settled` to `refunded`, and its bill (if any) reopened.

Why it matters beyond bookkeeping. A refunded obligation is no longer a live duplicate (`policy.ts:212`) and its `statement_ref` goes into `cameBack` (`orchestrator.ts:428-435`), which `matchPayPalHistory` excludes from "already paid" (`paypalHistory.ts:65-69`). If the clerk plants the real PayPal transaction id (they can read it from the real statement), the fake reversal both reopens the debt and silences PayPal's own record of the original payout, so the same job can be requested again and paid a second time. The repay is a new obligation that passes through policy; with system job-sheet backing under the auto threshold it needs no approver. I reproduced the refund, not the second payout.

Suggested fix.
1. Only an actor with `reconcile` may write `statement_ref`; a clerk's upload should display matches without persisting them (change `books.ts:206` to skip persistence when `!actorCan(by, "reconcile")`).
2. Only treat a stored `statement_ref` as an anchor when it was written from a `paypal_api` reconciliation (record the source on the settlement), or re-read the reversal from PayPal Transaction Search at apply time.
3. Require that a refund line's own transaction id has not been used before and that two people see a refund before it is applied.

### Low

#### SEC5-4: "Nothing moved" can be recorded without asking the provider (Should-fix)

`src/lib/engine/orchestrator.ts:1054-1056`. A person with `resolve_stuck` can mark an unknown attempt failed, after which `retryFailed` mints a new idempotency key and pays again. The "found" branch is checked against the provider (`externalIdMatchesKey`), but "nothing moved" is not. The reconcile action does call `findByIdempotencyKey` before replaying (`orchestrator.ts:1001-1010`). Fix: run `adapter.findByIdempotencyKey(attempt.idempotencyKey)` (PayPal) and the memo search (Stellar) inside the `nothing_moved` branch and refuse if the provider knows the key.

#### SEC5-5: PayPal duplicate check is single page and silently fails open (Should-fix before live)

`src/lib/ai/paypalTools.ts:92-105` reads one page of up to 500 transactions for 31 days and ignores pagination; `policy.ts:257` passes the rule with a note when PayPal cannot be read. A business with more than 500 transactions a month, or a PayPal outage, loses PayPal's side of the duplicate check without anyone being told on the payment page. Local history still runs. Fix: page through `next_page` or `total_pages`, and downgrade to "require approval with a visible warning" when the read fails or is incomplete.

#### SEC5-6: A returned PayPal payout can be retried to the same email without the "payout came back" warning (Should-fix)

`src/lib/rails/paypal.ts:30` maps item status RETURNED to `failed`; `retryFailed` (`orchestrator.ts:1064-1076`) then re-approves the same obligation, and `returnedToSameDetails` (`orchestrator.ts:542`) only looks at other obligations in state `refunded`. Cost is time rather than money (an unclaimed payout returns after 30 days), but it is the exact situation the rule exists for. Fix: treat RETURNED as an exception needing a person, or make the rule also consider this obligation's own earlier attempts.

#### SEC5-7: Contract hardening not covered by earlier IDs (Should-fix before deployment)

`contracts/spend-policy/src/lib.rs`. (a) The constructor and `set_limits` accept `cancel_window = 0` and an `owner` equal to the `agent` (`lib.rs:164-168`, `286-295`), which silently remove the two safeguards the README advertises. (b) `set_payee`, `set_limits`, `withdraw` and `resume` emit no events (`lib.rs:269-306`), so an off-chain monitor cannot alert on owner-side changes. (c) Pending proposals never expire and there is no bulk cancel, so a compromised agent can lock the daily cap with a handful of proposals up to the per-payment cap that the owner must cancel one by one (`lib.rs:189-200`, `242-255`). (d) Neither instance nor persistent entries are TTL-extended, so state can be archived and the contract stalls until someone restores it (liveness, not funds; `withdraw` has no storage write). Fix: validate a minimum window and `owner != agent`, emit events, add an expiry to proposals, and extend TTLs on use.

#### SEC5-11: Shared published credential, no password policy (Accept for the demo)

The hosted demo's single admin login is published for judges (README, Devpost). Anyone holding it can reset the demo, upload up to 500 MB of documents (`attachments.ts:10`, purged after 24 hours if unlinked), switch persona and change policy. Consistent with the stated design; fill-the-disk is the realistic abuse on a free instance. For live use add the password-length check under SEC5-3.

### Info

- **SEC5-10: Document upload over HTTP answered "No file received."** from `uploadEvidenceAction` for multipart posts built by curl, Python and Node (`FormData`), identical to what `NewObligationForm.tsx:55-58` sends. ## 5. Re-check of the four money-path rules in CONTRIBUTING

1. **The AI has no path to a payment tool.** Holds. The model's tools are `lookup_vendor`, `read_evidence`, `vendor_history`, three fixed read-only PayPal GETs with validated arguments, and `submit_interpretation` (`agent.ts:66-127`, `paypalTools.ts:52-84`). `src/lib/ai` imports `rails/paypalAuth` for a token only. The agent shares the PayPal app that has Payouts scope (documented known gap); every call it can make is a GET with a fixed path.
2. **Policy is a pure function and is re-run inside execution.** Holds with one nit (SEC5-9). `executeObligation` refreshes PayPal history, re-runs `runPolicy`, refuses unless the decision is `auto_execute` (`orchestrator.ts:831-838`), reloads the obligation, re-checks the payout hash outside and inside the transaction (`:850`, `:873`) and refuses if an earlier attempt has not definitively failed (`:874-878`). Autopilot goes through the same function (`autopilot.ts:112`).
3. **Every attempt carries an idempotency key.** Holds for all four rails: PayPal `sender_batch_id` plus `PayPal-Request-Id` (`paypal.ts:84, 104`), Stellar memo with a prior-payment search and the signed hash persisted before submission (`stellar.ts:105-134`), Wise `customerTransactionId`, bank file line `idempotency_key` (`bankFile.ts:33-36`). The key is `sha256(obligationId:attemptNo)` (`ids.ts:17-19`); unknown outcomes keep the same key and never mint a new one (`orchestrator.ts:979-1021`), apart from SEC5-4.
4. **Webhooks only trigger a re-check.** Holds. The route stores the event under a unique id, looks up the attempt by batch id and calls `confirmSettlement`, which polls the rail (`route.ts:87-90`, `orchestrator.ts:1079-1117`). In mock mode it returns 404 (verified). In live mode it needs all five PayPal headers and a `verify-webhook-signature` SUCCESS, and fails closed without `PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID` (`paypal.ts:203-229`). A processing error answers 500 so PayPal retries (the unit test covers it). Old events can be replayed with a valid signature (no timestamp window), which only causes another status poll.

## 6. Agent prompt-injection surface

Untrusted text reaches the model through the raw request (`agent.ts:132`), `read_evidence` (records and up to 6,000 characters of every attached document, `agent.ts:85`), `vendor_history` descriptions, PayPal transaction notes and, for uploaded photos or PDFs in live mode, the document reader (`documents.ts:171-196`). The system prompts tell the model to treat all of it as data, to flag instructions found inside it, and to call `submit_interpretation` once.

What injected text can change: the vendor the model selects (from the known list only, `agent.ts:175`), the amount, currency, reference, confidence, flags, notes and a free-text summary. All fields are clamped and shape-checked by `normalise` (`agent.ts:174-198`).

What it cannot change, by construction:
- It cannot reduce a deterministic requirement. A payee the request text does not name raises `request_names_vendor`; evidence that does not name the payee in full raises `evidence_names_vendor`; an amount that disagrees with evidence blocks; vendor-uploaded evidence warns; each warning forces a person (`policy.ts:80-117, 137-176, 318-324`). The model's flags and low confidence can only add warnings.
- It cannot create independent support. Only a "system" job sheet, booking or purchase order, or a rate-card match, counts (`policy.ts:346`), and neither is derived from model output. A vendor naming a departure code in their invoice only helps if the business already listed that vendor on that departure, and a repeat of that departure is a duplicate.
- It cannot move money: a changed vendor, amount, currency or reference supersedes existing approvals (`orchestrator.ts:196-201`).

Residual: a misleading AI summary shown to an approver (displayed as plain escaped text; I found no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` or `innerHTML` anywhere in `src`), the agent's amount-mismatch flags being discarded when a payment is checked against a bill (`policy.ts:293`, intended and bounded by the bill's open balance), and the fallback to the rule-based reader when the model call fails (`agent.ts:54-60`, it adds a visible flag). The hosted demo uses the rule-based reader, so there is no model to inject. The practical weakness in this area is SEC5-1, an insider rather than injected text.

## 7. npm audit and dependency analysis

`npm audit` (all dependencies): **4 moderate**, one root advisory.
`npm audit --omit=dev`: **0 vulnerabilities**.

The advisory is esbuild <= 0.24.2, GHSA-67mh-4wv8-2f99 ("any website can send requests to the esbuild development server and read the response"). It is reported on four nodes of a single chain: `drizzle-kit@0.31.11` > `@esbuild-kit/esm-loader@2.6.5` > `@esbuild-kit/core-utils@3.3.2` > `esbuild@0.18.20`.

How I decided whether it can run in the deployed app:
1. `npm ls esbuild --all` shows the vulnerable 0.18.20 only under `drizzle-kit`, a devDependency. `tsx@4.23.15` (used by `npm run db:seed`, which `render.yaml` runs at start) and `vitest` / `vite` use esbuild 0.28.2, outside the affected range.
2. `grep` shows `drizzle-kit` imported only by `drizzle.config.ts`; nothing under `src/` or `scripts/` imports it or esbuild.
3. The advisory concerns esbuild's `serve` dev server. Neither drizzle-kit's CLI nor this app starts it, and no server runs esbuild at request time. The Next.js production bundle does not include esbuild.
4. `npm audit --omit=dev` independently confirms no production dependency is affected.

Verdict: build and development tooling only, not reachable in the deployed app. **Accept.** The audit's suggested `npm audit fix --force` would downgrade drizzle-kit to 0.18.1, which is wrong; wait for a drizzle-kit release that drops `@esbuild-kit`. One observation: `render.yaml` starts the service with `npm run db:seed`, which uses tsx (a devDependency), so the deployed image carries dev packages on disk (including esbuild 0.18.20). That does not make the advisory reachable, but it is why `--omit=dev` is a statement about what runs, not about what is installed.

The earlier postcss advisory (G9) is gone: `package.json` overrides postcss to ^8.5.32. The lockfile resolves every package from `registry.npmjs.org` (614 resolved URLs, none elsewhere). No secrets or key files are committed, and history contains only `.env.example`.

## 8. Soroban contract review (source only, plus `cargo test`)

`cargo test --locked` passes 11 of 11. Reasoning from the source:

Sound: `propose` needs the agent's auth, rejects pause, non-positive amounts, long memos, duplicate ids, payees not on the owner's allow-list, amounts over the per-payment cap and anything that would push released plus pending plus new over the daily cap (`lib.rs:171-203`). `release` repeats every check at release time, sets state before the transfer so a failed transfer rolls back, and uses overflow-checked arithmetic (`Cargo.toml` `overflow-checks = true`). `cancel`, `resume`, `set_payee`, `set_limits` and `withdraw` need the owner's auth; `pause` needs the owner or the agent and verifies `by` against both before `require_auth` (`lib.rs:258-267`). A payment id can only be used once, including after cancellation.

Open or new: SEC2-9 (Spends growth in instance storage: with 1-stroop proposals, each released by anyone after the window, the vector grows within the 24 hour window until the instance entry reaches the ledger entry size limit, after which writes to instance storage fail and `cancel`, `pause` and `set_limits`, which all write instance storage, may fail with it; `withdraw` still works, which limits the damage to a freeze of controls for up to a day at a time), SEC2-10 (no agent rotation, no TTL), SEC2-11 (no minimum cancel window), and SEC5-7. Fix for SEC2-9: a minimum payment amount, a hard cap on entries, or 24 hourly bucket entries in persistent storage instead of an unbounded vector.

The contract is not deployed and not called by the app, so none of this is live exposure today.

## 9. Deployment and hosted demo (`render.yaml`)

Good: simulated rails and rule-based reader; logins required (the app answers 503 rather than running open without them, `middleware.ts:70-73`); `CHECKRAIL_DATA_KEY` generated by Render; webhook route does not exist in mock mode; HSTS, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, CSP with `frame-ancestors 'none'` and `form-action 'self'` verified on responses; attachments served with a sandboxing CSP and `nosniff`; personas are owner-only and switched off when `PAYPAL_ENV=live` or `STELLAR_NETWORK=public`.

Weak but accepted for a judging demo: one shared admin credential (SEC5-11); personas on, so any judge can act as every role; the idle reset (SEC2-5); the free plan's ephemeral disk; `CHECKRAIL_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` unset (SEC5-3); the autopilot heartbeat running inside the single web process; and `script-src 'unsafe-inline'` (required by Next's bootstrap, noted as I1 in round 4).

## 10. Things checked and found sound

- Authentication gate on every path including static-looking API routes; header stripping and overwrite in the middleware; owner-only personas; real login recorded on audit rows made through a persona.
- Server-action origin check (cross-origin post rejected); no custom auth cookie to steal (Basic auth plus a `httpOnly`, `sameSite=lax` persona cookie).
- Public/internal action boundary: every internal action refuses on a public path, because `currentActor` rejects `x-checkrail-public` and the middleware strips identity there.
- Role matrix: accountant cannot send, approve or reset; clerk cannot approve, verify, change autonomy, team, tax or period lock; engine functions re-check permissions themselves (`requirePermission` in `bills`, `runs`, `vendors`, `vendorLinks`, `bankFiles`, `books`, `rates`).
- Concurrency: compare-and-swap state transitions, immediate transaction around attempt insert and state change, open-attempt guard, payout hash pinned at approval and re-checked in the lock.
- Dual approval (two logins, covering approver first, capped approver as second signer), one approval per login, approvals superseded on amount, payee, reference, evidence or payout changes, new warnings since approval sending the payment back.
- Vendor payout changes: operator or vendor proposal, second person verifies, requester cannot verify while someone else can; `country`, tax form, withholding, retainer and phone changes need `verify_vendor`.
- PayPal adapter: failure classification (4xx fail, timeout and 5xx unknown), duplicate-batch recovery that only trusts a batch whose `sender_batch_id` is ours, item-level status before confirming, UNCLAIMED and ONHOLD kept as exceptions, fail-closed webhook verification.
- Stellar adapter: sequence-number argument for in-flight duplicates, history search before send, hash stored before submission, USD-only, destination not the treasury or issuer, network and issuer consistency checks.
- Bank account numbers: AES-256-GCM, random IV, key from environment, public development key refused on live rails and on non-demo production, masked everywhere I looked, absent from logs and the audit table, bank file download restricted to `pay` and audited (but see SEC5-2).
- Exports: CSV cells neutralise formula prefixes (`record.ts:128-136`); bank-file text is stripped to a safe charset; no XSS sinks in `src`.
- Uploads: allow-list of types, 10 MB cap, 500 MB total, text rendered as `text/plain`, sandboxing CSP on non-PDF, unlinked uploads purged; PDF parsed in a time- and memory-limited child.
- Tick route: bearer token compared with a constant-time function when set, custom header required otherwise, 403 for live rails without a token.
- Secrets: none committed.
